# Catalog expansion: geological sources

These sources document geological context, not calibrated ASI memberships, measured
climate fallbacks, authorized test campaigns or habitat suitability. Coordinates and
elevations identify approximate representative terrain, not surveyed sampling sites.
Testbeds describe proposed experiments. Live DEM and POWER provenance is retained
in each generated record.

- **fernandina-caldera**: [Fernandina Basaltic Caldera](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=353010). Deep collapse caldera and basaltic shield flows offer volcanic morphology and remote terrain-mapping analogs. Elevation represents the caldera interior, not the 1476 m summit; active eruptions, unstable walls and protected ecosystems preclude assuming safe access.
- **alcedo-hydrothermal**: [Alcedo Hydrothermal Caldera](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=353040). Fumarolic alteration in a Galapagos shield with both basalt and rhyolite provides an early-Mars hydrothermal comparison. Alcedo is not uniformly basaltic; fumaroles, humid weathering and protected wildlife restrict sampling. The point represents the caldera, not a verified vent.
- **cayambe-glacier**: [Cayambe Glacier Margins](https://www2.igepn.edu.ec/cayambe). Equatorial glacier margins and volcanic debris provide ice-rock interface and cold-terrain mobility scenarios. The representative western glacier flank is below the summit; temperate glacier ice and andesitic-dacitic rocks differ from lunar cold traps and basaltic Mars.
- **altar-crater-lake**: [El Altar Crater Lake - Laguna Amarilla](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=352824). Glacially modified volcanic amphitheater and Laguna Amarilla offer crater-basin sediment and ice-retreat analogs. Glacial erosion contributes to the basin morphology; a modern meltwater lake is neither an impact crater nor evidence of a Martian-style paleolake.
- **payunia-volcanic**: [La Payunia Volcanic Field](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5615/). Extensive basalt flows and scoria cones support comparisons of planetary fissure volcanism and rough-surface traverses. Regional volcanism includes evolved compositions; tube access is unverified and terrestrial weathering differs from lunar regolith.
- **salar-atacama-pajonales**: [Salar de Pajonales - Atacama Region](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-022-01882-x). Gypsum-bearing salt crusts and microbial habitats studied for biosignature search strategies provide an evaporitic Mars analog. Pajonales is a separate salar south of Salar de Atacama; modern microbes and intermittent water do not imply life on Mars.
- **nevado-tres-cruces**: [Nevado Tres Cruces High Volcanic Flank](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=355862). Cold arid volcanic debris below the Tres Cruces summits offers high-altitude frost and regolith-mobility scenarios. Point represents the western flank, not a summit or the national-park lagoons; permafrost is a prior requiring ground validation.
- **tatacoa-badlands**: [Tatacoa Sedimentary Badlands](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2020.587022). Eroded Miocene sedimentary strata and gullies support layered-terrain navigation and fluvial-stratigraphy comparisons. Tatacoa is a tropical dry-forest badland, not a hyperarid desert; rainfall, fossils and soil biology differ from modern Mars.
- **lencois-maranhenses**: [Lencois Maranhenses Coastal Dunes](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1611). Migrating quartz-sand dunes and interdune lagoons offer aeolian bedform and wet-dry mobility comparisons. This rainy coastal dune field is not a desert; quartz sand, seasonal freshwater and terrestrial air density differ from Martian dunes.
- **pariaqaqa-permafrost**: [Pariaqaqa Periglacial Terrain](https://publicaciones.unirioja.es/ojs/index.php/cig/en/article/view/4331). Documented glacier retreat and morainic terrain in the Pariacaca range motivate frost-weathering and ice-debris exploration scenarios. Permafrost membership is an unmeasured scenario prior, not confirmed local frozen-ground extent; tropical glaciers and seasonal melt differ from planetary ice deposits.
- **poas-acid-crater**: [Poas Acid Crater Lake](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=345040). Acidic crater-lake fluids and sulfur-rich hydrothermal alteration provide mineralogical and extremophile comparisons for early Mars. Lake extent and chemistry vary with activity; corrosive gases, eruptions and a wet tropical climate limit operational transfer.
- **kilauea-caldera**: [Kilauea Summit Caldera](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea). Basaltic summit collapse structures and lava surfaces provide volcanic mapping and planetary flow-emplacement comparisons. Active vents, gases and changing crater geometry prevent assuming traversable ground; this caldera point does not locate an accessible lava tube.
- **mount-etna**: [Mount Etna Basaltic Flank](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1427/). Basaltic lava flows, tephra and volcanic conduits offer planetary rover mobility and flow-morphology scenarios. The point represents a high southern flank rather than the changing summit; snow, active eruptions and unverified tube access constrain comparisons.
- **white-sands-gypsum**: [White Sands Gypsum Dunes](https://www.nps.gov/whsa/learn/geology-of-white-sands.htm). Gypsum dunes connect evaporite sediment supply with aeolian transport, supporting Martian sulfate-dune comparisons. Shallow groundwater and terrestrial wind thresholds influence dune cohesion; protected habitats and archaeological resources constrain testing.
- **channeled-scablands**: [Channeled Scablands - Dry Falls](https://home.nps.gov/iafl/learn/index.htm). Catastrophic glacial-flood channels cut into Columbia River basalt provide process comparisons for Martian outflow channels. Flood scale, gravity and sediment supply differ from Mars; the Dry Falls point samples one part of a large regional drainage network.
- **wadi-rum**: [Wadi Rum Sandstone Desert](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1377/). Sandstone cliffs, joints and dune corridors support desert navigation and sedimentary outcrop mapping scenarios. Sandstone weathering and terrestrial wind transport are not basaltic Mars geology; cultural heritage and protected landscapes limit deployment.
- **makgadikgadi-pans**: [Makgadikgadi Salt Pans](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5559/). Remnant paleolake floors, salt crusts and seasonal inundation offer evaporite-basin and desiccation-pattern analogs. Seasonal flooding, wildlife and biologically active sediments differ from dry Martian paleolakes; crust bearing strength varies sharply.
- **lake-tyrrell**: [Lake Tyrrell Saline Sediments](https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/nai/annual-reports/2007/cal/iron-and-sulfur-based-biospheres-and-their-biosignatures/index.html). Hypersaline sediments and locally acidic iron-rich groundwater discharge support Mars analog studies of iron-sulfur cycling and biosignatures. Acidity varies across the lake; living microbial mats, shallow brines and temperate weather are not modern Martian conditions.
- **sudbury-basin**: [Sudbury Impact Basin](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/sudbury-impact-structure-148844/). Ancient impact-melt rocks and basin deposits support comparisons of large-impact differentiation and post-impact alteration. Erosion, tectonic deformation, vegetation and mining obscure the original crater; this is not a pristine lunar basin surface.
- **ries-crater**: [Nordlinger Ries Impact Crater](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-designates-8-new-global-geoparks). Suevite impact breccias and preserved crater-basin morphology support shock-metamorphism and ejecta-mapping comparisons. Sedimentary target rocks, later lake deposits and cultivated land differ from lunar maria; outcrop access requires local arrangements.
- **vredefort-dome**: [Vredefort Impact Dome](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1162/). Deeply eroded central uplift and shocked ancient crust document large-impact deformation relevant to planetary basin interiors. The dome exposes deep crust after prolonged erosion; it is not the original crater rim or an intact lunar-style basin.
- **tassili-najjer**: [Tassili nAjjer Sandstone Plateau](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/179/). Eroded sandstone plateaus, rock arches and dry valleys support layered-terrain mapping and desert route-planning analogs. Sandstone erosion combines inherited wet-climate processes with modern aridity; archaeological and ecological protection limits field testing.
- **waw-an-namus**: [Waw an Namus Volcanic Caldera](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/desert-contrast-86054/). Dark basaltic tephra surrounding a Saharan caldera provides volcanic-ash transport and spectral-contrast analogs. Oasis lakes and vegetation occur within the depression; volcanic age and local access are uncertain, and dark deposits do not establish lava tubes.
- **jeju-manjanggul**: [Jeju Manjanggul Lava Tube](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1264/). A documented basaltic lava-tube system offers conduit geometry, robot localization and subsurface communication analogs. Humid cave conditions, biological colonization and protected access limit shelter transfer; tube relevance is not a claim of habitat readiness.
- **undara-lava-tubes**: [Undara Lava Tube System](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/undara-volcanic/about). Long basaltic lava conduits with roof-collapse sections provide geometry and cave-robot navigation analogs. Seasonal moisture, bat habitats, vegetation and roof stability require assessment; cave shelter priors do not establish lunar habitat suitability.

## Ranking verification

With unchanged legacy priors and the existing scoring formula, the live POWER
climatology retrieved for this expansion yields Mare Tranquillitatis scores of
89.10 for `mauna-loa-hiseas` and 87.49 for `galapagos-lava-tubes`, in first and
second place respectively. These also match the pre-expansion checked-in data;
the requested 89.12 / 87.52 values are not reproduced by these live inputs.
No scores or telemetry were overridden to force those values.

All 25 additions have an upper bound below 87.5 even with ideal target-matching
air temperatures (18/16 C) and zero precipitation; the highest bound is 83.45
for Undara. The engine regression test checks this climate-independent bound;
the frontend tests verify the published ranking, live provenance and translations.
